## Daydream Atlanta: Running the show

I was the lead organizer for **Daydream Atlanta**, a free, teen-led two-day game jam at Georgia Tech and part of Hack Club’s worldwide Daydream weekend. Nearly 90 students came together to learn, build, and demo games they shipped to itch.io.

I enjoy creating video games and being part of creative technical communities. Daydream Atlanta was a chance to make that community tangible: a welcoming space for students—especially first-time game developers—to turn an idea into something playable and share it with other people.

### At a glance

- **Dates:** Sept 27-28, 2025
- **Venue:** Georgia Tech Van Leer Building (Electrical and Computer Engineering)
- **Format:** In-person, teen-led, free (food, swag, prizes)
- **Focus:** Learn by building: lots of first-time game devs using **Godot**
- **My role:** Lead organizer

### Partners & sponsors

**We Create Tech**, **NSBE @ Georgia Tech**, **Women In Tech**, **Exploding Kittens**, **Jukebox**, **VMotiv8**, and **YRI Fellowship** helped make the event possible.

## The Weekend

The room stayed busy with teams learning Godot, working through bugs, and helping each other ship.

creative problem-solving experience I wanted for the event.

## What I wanted to make

Daydream is a worldwide, teen-run game jam built around workshops, hacking, and demos.
My goal was to give Atlanta students a low-pressure way into game development:
learn by building, find collaborators, and leave with something real they could play and share.

We prioritized Godot because it was accessible for beginners, but teams were free to use the engines and tools that fit their games.
Every project needed to be playable on itch.io so it could be shared and judged easily.

## I joined in, too

While preparing for Daydream, I built and released **Yeastbound: Rise of the Dough** so I could learn Godot by making a game myself.
It placed **15th overall** out of roughly **190** Daydream Global entries, and Hack Club highlighted it as **#2 in Audio**.
